# Engagement evidence

What research says keeps a reader with ADHD engaged with self-paced material, graded by evidence
strength. Both `fun-briefs` and `fun-grilling` design from this table; it is their single source of
truth. Full findings (56 sources, effect sizes, verification notes):
https://github.com/calindumitru/funvenio/blob/research/adhd-learning-engagement/research/adhd-learning-engagement-2026-10-30/findings.md
(October 2026).

**The caveat that bounds everything here:** no controlled study has tested adults with ADHD reading
documents or e-learning. The principles combine ADHD cognitive findings (mostly from children) with
instructional-design research on everyone. Say "evidence suggests", never "proven for ADHD".

**The core mechanism:** people with ADHD discount delayed rewards more steeply (d = 0.43, adults as
much as children), find boredom harder (r = 0.40), and hold less in working memory. The attention gap
is present from the first minute rather than fading fast. So the lever is **a near payoff**, not
merely shorter text.

## The myths, in one line each

- Bionic Reading, dyslexia fonts, coloured overlays: no better than plain text; one study found a cost.
- "Dopamine hits": rests on one correlational scan study; what holds is behavioural (sooner, more frequent payoffs).
- Fidget spinners: worsened attention in young children with ADHD. Ordinary movement is different.
- "ADHD attention collapses after minutes": the deficit is mostly constant over time.
- Pomodoro and body doubling: no trial with ADHD participants (low-risk to offer, never to claim).
- "Rewards always help": expected rewards for completing a task lower later interest (d ≈ −0.28 to −0.40); informational positive feedback raises it (+0.33).
- "Progress bars keep people going": steady bars don't reduce drop-off; slow-starting ones increase it.
- Gamification: g = 0.49 on learning on average, but a 16-week gamified course lowered motivation and exam scores, and novelty fades after about four weeks.
